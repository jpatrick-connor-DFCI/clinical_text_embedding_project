# Manuscript figure captions

These legends accompany Figures 1–4 and the within-cancer supplemental figures. The renderer appends data-derived sample sizes and model statistics, and records whether the configured endpoint filter is enabled. Final submission copies are written to `CLINICAL_FIGURES_OUT/captions/`.

**Figure 1. Cohort composition, data availability, and prediction endpoints.**

(a) Cancer-type distribution among patients with cancer-type annotations and clinical text; counts and percentages use the same cohort denominator. Other combines less frequent cancer types. (b) Major cancer-stage distribution among patients with a recognized stage (I-IV). (c) Cumulative cohort selection as successive data-availability criteria are applied; each box is a subset of the preceding box. Repeated counts indicate criteria without additional attrition. PRS, polygenic risk scores; somatic, somatic genomic features. (d) Numbers of available prediction endpoints in the mortality/metastasis, three-character ICD-10, four-character ICD-10, and PhecodeX schemes. Endpoint counts are not patient counts, and related endpoints across coding schemes are not mutually exclusive. Panel-specific denominators are shown in the artwork.

**Figure 2. Discrimination and survival stratification using clinical text.**

(a) Held-out Harrell's concordance index (C-index) for the base model, containing age, sex, and cancer type, versus the model additionally incorporating clinical-text embeddings. Each point represents an endpoint; the diagonal indicates equal discrimination. (b) Endpoint-level differences in C-index (text minus base), grouped by outcome scheme. Positive values favor the text model. Diamonds and bars indicate the median and interquartile range; violin densities are omitted for groups with fewer than ten endpoints. Endpoint counts are shown above each group. Endpoints are correlated, so these summaries are descriptive. (c,d) Kaplan-Meier estimates of overall survival from first treatment in the known-stage cohort, stratified by major cancer stage (c) or quartiles of the text-derived mortality-risk score (d). Quartiles are defined across this cohort, with Q1 the lowest and Q4 the highest risk. Shading denotes pointwise 95% confidence intervals. Displayed C-indices describe the corresponding stage or risk-score predictor. P values in c and d are from log-rank tests. ICD-10, International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision.

**Figure 3. Relative predictive performance and joint associations across data modalities.**

(a) Points indicate mean within-endpoint C-index ranks (1 = best); horizontal bars indicate interquartile ranges across endpoints. Ranking requires complete modality-performance results and is restricted to endpoints represented in the joint Cox analysis; its endpoint count may therefore be smaller than in the other panels. (b) Numbers of endpoints with a significant modality coefficient in the unpenalized joint Cox model. Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment is applied across modalities within each endpoint; significance is defined as false discovery rate below 0.05. (c) Distributions of joint Cox coefficients per one-standard-deviation increase in each modality's risk score. Positive coefficients indicate higher hazard conditional on the other modality scores. Points represent endpoints; diamonds and bars indicate the median and interquartile range. For display in c, coefficients outside 1.5 interquartile ranges of the first and third quartiles are omitted separately for each modality. Panels b and c use the same joint-model endpoint set before display trimming. Endpoints are correlated; summaries across endpoints are descriptive. PRS, polygenic risk scores; met. burden, metastatic burden.

**Figure 4. Mortality-risk dynamics and survival across cancer stages.**

(a) Longitudinal text-derived mortality-risk scores standardized within each observation month; colors are truncated at z = -2.5 and +2.5. Rows represent patients and columns represent months. Up to 500 patients per dynamics group are displayed, with shown/full cohort sizes labeled. Falling, stable, and rising groups are ordered by mean fitted risk-score slope. (b) Kaplan-Meier overall survival by dynamics group, conditional on survival to the indicated landmark. (c) Dynamics-group proportions among patients with each recognized stage and an assigned group. (d) Conditional overall survival for stage I-II patients with rising risk versus stage IV patients with falling risk. Legend counts in d report patients in each comparison group after landmark eligibility filtering. In b and d, shading denotes pointwise 95% confidence intervals; survival estimates use delayed entry at the landmark. Panel denominators differ with trajectory, survival, and stage-data availability.

**Supplemental Figure S2. Within-cancer discrimination of text and base models.**

Held-out Harrell's concordance index (C-index) of the existing pooled text and base models, evaluated separately within each recorded cancer type using matched patient scores from the full-cohort risk-score outputs. Each point is an endpoint, colored by coding family; the diagonal indicates equal discrimination. Facet annotations report the number of eligible endpoints and median paired text-minus-base C-index difference. C-indices are computed within joint text/base outer-fold blocks and combined using comparable-pair counts, so patients scored by different fitted models are not compared. Eligibility requires at least 20 matched patients, 5 events, and 1 comparable pair per endpoint/cancer comparison by default; the patient and event thresholds are configurable. Rendering retains eligible comparisons and applies the manuscript endpoint filter, which by default excludes endpoints whose pooled text-minus-base C-index difference falls outside the pooled mean ± 3 standard deviations. Cancer types are shown across pages of at most 12. Endpoints are correlated, and these summaries are descriptive; no endpoint-level significance tests are performed.

**Supplemental Figure S3. Within-cancer discrimination of text versus other data modalities.**

Heatmap of median paired text-minus-comparator C-index differences, with rows representing recorded cancer types and columns representing the five other modalities. The existing pooled models are evaluated on matched patient scores from the held-out risk-score outputs. Each cell reports the median text C-index, median comparator C-index, median paired difference, and number of eligible endpoints. The median paired difference is calculated across endpoint-level differences and need not equal the difference of the two median C-indices. Positive differences favor text; unavailable comparisons are marked and are not assigned zero. C-indices are computed within joint text/comparator outer-fold blocks and combined using comparable-pair counts to avoid comparing patients scored by different fitted models. Eligibility requires at least 20 matched patients, 5 events, and 1 comparable pair per endpoint/cancer/comparator by default; the patient and event thresholds are configurable. The same manuscript endpoint filter as Supplemental Figure S2 is applied. Matching is pair-specific, so eligible patients and endpoints can differ across modalities. Cancer types are shown across pages of at most 12. Endpoints are correlated, and these summaries are descriptive; no endpoint-level significance tests are performed. PRS, polygenic risk scores; met. burden, metastatic burden.

**Supplemental Figure S3 (ranks). Modality rank within cancer types.**

Mean within-endpoint C-index rank (1 = best; ties share the average rank) of each modality within each cancer type, with interquartile ranges in brackets; rows report the number of ranked endpoints. For each endpoint and cancer type, all modalities are evaluated on the same patients (those with every modality's held-out prediction, an outcome and a cancer label) and the same comparable pairs, with Harrell's C-index computed within blocks sharing every modality's outer fold and combined using comparable-pair counts. An endpoint is ranked only when every modality is evaluable (complete case), as in Figure 3a. The same eligibility thresholds and manuscript endpoint filter as Supplemental Figure S2 apply. A version restricted to breast, leukemia, lung, bowel, brain, skin, pancreas, lymphoma and cancer of unknown primary (CUP) is also provided. Endpoints are correlated; summaries are descriptive.

**Supplemental Figure S3 (joint Cox). Joint Cox model within selected cancer types.**

The Figure 3 joint Cox model refitted separately within breast, leukemia, lung, bowel, brain, skin, pancreas, lymphoma and CUP. For each endpoint and cancer type, held-out modality risk scores are standardized within the cancer type and entered jointly into an unpenalized Cox model; fits require at least 20 patients, 5 events, 5 non-events and two non-constant modalities, and fits with any coefficient magnitude above 5 are discarded. An endpoint is included for a cancer type only when every modality fitted for that cancer type is present. (Violins) Coefficients per one-standard-deviation increase in each modality's risk score; diamonds and bars indicate the median and interquartile range, and coefficients outside 1.5 interquartile ranges are omitted from display only. (Heatmap) Endpoints with a significant coefficient (Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate below 0.05 across modalities within each endpoint's fit) over eligible endpoints, with the number of positive significant coefficients in parentheses. The manuscript endpoint filter is applied. Endpoints are correlated; summaries are descriptive.

**Supplemental Figure (overall survival). Overall-survival discrimination within selected cancer types.**

Overall-survival C-index of the existing pooled models within breast, leukemia, lung, bowel, brain, skin, pancreas, lymphoma and CUP. Each panel pairs text with one comparator (base, from full-cohort scores; other modalities, from held-out feature-comparison scores); segments join the comparator and text C-indices, and right-hand values give their difference. Each comparison uses its own matched patients, so text C-indices can differ between panels. C-indices are computed within joint outer-fold blocks and combined using comparable-pair counts; eligibility thresholds match Supplemental Figure S2.
